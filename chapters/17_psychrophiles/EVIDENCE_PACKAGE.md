# Psychrophiles evidence package

This file supports later chapter drafting. It follows the approved architecture in `SOURCE_AUDIT.md`, records only evidence judged useful for that architecture, and uses citation keys from `book/references.bib`.

## 1. Executive editorial summary

The central scientific argument is well supported: cooling changes reaction kinetics, diffusion, viscosity, phase behaviour, membrane state and macromolecular dynamics; cold-adapted microorganisms compensate through multiple, non-universal changes whose value depends on the organism and its environment [@damico2006; @demaayer2014]. The operational definition introduced by Morita remains useful—minimum growth temperature at or below 0 °C, optimum at or below 15 °C and maximum near 20 °C—but should be presented as a historical convention, not a natural boundary [@morita1975; @cavicchioli2016]. “Psychrotolerant” is the preferred broad term for organisms that grow in the cold but have substantially warmer optima; “psychrotroph” remains common in food microbiology and older literature.

The lecture's lower-limit claim requires separation. *Planococcus halocryophilus* Or1 grew in a liquid, high-solute medium at −15 °C, with an estimated generation time of about 50 days; the same study detected radiolabelled-acetate mineralisation at −25 °C in inoculated sterile-permafrost microcosms over 210 days [@mykytczuk2013]. The latter demonstrates low metabolic activity, not division. Earlier acetate incorporation in natural permafrost at −20 °C likewise supports activity but not population growth [@rivkina2000]. No universal lower temperature for life follows from these results because phase state, solutes, duration and the measured endpoint differ.

The membrane and enzyme modules are strong but need qualification. Homeoviscous responses are diverse and lineage-specific; even Archaea can alter ether-lipid unsaturation, but one archaeal example cannot define a universal rule [@nichols2004]. Cold-active enzymes often exchange stability or affinity for catalytic competence through altered conformational dynamics, commonly near the active site, but “greater whole-protein flexibility” and amino-acid recipes are unsafe generalisations [@feller2003]. RNA helicases and RNA-binding proteins are credible components of cold adaptation, yet expression or gene presence alone does not establish adaptive function [@lim2000].

The lecture's “antifreeze” category should be replaced by distinct ice-binding, ice-nucleating, compatible-solute and EPS mechanisms. *Marinomonas primoryensis* provides direct evidence for ice adhesion, not simply freezing-point depression [@bardolev2016; @guo2017]. *Colwellia psychrerythraea* 34H provides useful but bounded evidence that EPS production changes under cold/saline conditions and that native EPS can be preferentially retained in experimental saline ice [@marx2009; @ewert2011].

Rejected material includes rigid taxonomic categories, universal lipid or protein recipes, hyphenated physiotype catalogues, the combined −15 to 150 °C “life span”, gene-presence-as-function claims, unidentified supernatant proteins presented as antifreeze proteins, and broad application catalogues. Overall confidence is high for the causal architecture, moderate to high for the selected mechanisms, and moderate for lower-limit and extracellular-matrix interpretations.

## 2. Reading priority

### Essential

1. **Morita 1975** [@morita1975] — source of the conventional cardinal-temperature definition and the terminology problem.
2. **Cavicchioli 2016** [@cavicchioli2016] — essential corrective: laboratory growth-rate optima and maxima do not fully describe environmental adaptation.
3. **D'Amico et al. 2006** [@damico2006] — compact mechanistic synthesis linking physical consequences to membranes, enzymes, nucleic acids and extracellular protection.
4. **De Maayer et al. 2014** [@demaayer2014] — broader synthesis of physiology and genomic hypotheses; use critically because many omics claims are associative.
5. **Mykytczuk et al. 2013** [@mykytczuk2013] — key primary case for growth at −15 °C and activity at −25 °C, including the experimental conditions that make the distinction interpretable.
6. **Feller and Gerday 2003** [@feller2003] — clearest source for the activity–stability–flexibility model and its thermodynamic logic.
7. **Deming 2002** [@deming2002] — best conceptual bridge from cold to liquid microhabitats, salinity, pressure and polar ecology.

### Supporting

1. **Rivkina et al. 2000** [@rivkina2000] — primary evidence for acetate incorporation into lipids in permafrost down to −20 °C; supports activity, not reproduction.
2. **Nichols et al. 2004** [@nichols2004] — direct archaeal lipid evidence from *Methanococcoides burtonii*.
3. **Lim et al. 2000** [@lim2000] — biochemical and regulatory characterisation of a low-temperature-regulated archaeal DEAD-box RNA helicase.
4. **Bar-Dolev et al. 2016** [@bardolev2016] and **Guo et al. 2017** [@guo2017] — direct functional and structural evidence for the *Marinomonas primoryensis* ice adhesin.
5. **Marx et al. 2009** [@marx2009] and **Ewert and Deming 2011** [@ewert2011] — bounded primary evidence for *Colwellia* EPS production and interaction with experimental saline ice.
6. **Rahman et al. 2024** [@rahman2024] — optional frontier example; valuable mainly for teaching why an observed supernatant effect does not identify the active molecule.

### Background

1. **Lauro and Cavicchioli 2013** [@lauro2013] — broad ecology and adaptation synthesis; useful for perspective, not detailed regional claims.
2. **Dolev et al. 2016** [@dolev2016] — authoritative terminology and mechanistic context for ice-binding proteins.

## 3. Verified terminology

| Term | Recommended editorial usage | Nuance |
|---|---|---|
| **psychrophile** | An organism adapted to growth in persistently cold conditions. Introduce Morita's operational convention: minimum at or below 0 °C, optimum at or below 15 °C, and maximum near 20 °C [@morita1975]. | Treat the thresholds as historical and operational. Ecological adaptation cannot be inferred from optimum temperature alone [@cavicchioli2016]. |
| **psychrotolerant** | An organism able to grow at low temperature but with an optimum substantially above the conventional psychrophile range. | Preferred general contrast with psychrophile. Tolerance spans a continuum; do not imply a clean natural boundary. |
| **psychrotroph** | Use mainly when discussing food microbiology or literature that defines organisms by growth under refrigeration. | Often used as a synonym for psychrotolerant, but criteria vary by discipline. Define the author's usage if retained. |
| **growth** | Net increase in biomass, cell number or a validated population proxy under stated conditions. | Increased optical density alone may be ambiguous in freezing or precipitating media; viable counts, microscopy or another independent measure strengthen the inference. |
| **reproduction** | Completion of cell division producing new cells. | Use when division itself or an unambiguous increase in viable cell number is demonstrated. It is the strongest endpoint in lower-limit claims. |
| **maintenance** | Energy-consuming preservation or repair of cellular integrity without demonstrated net population growth. | Maintenance may sustain viability over long intervals and must not be relabelled as growth. It is difficult to measure directly in environmental samples. |
| **metabolic activity** | A measured biochemical transformation attributable to cells, such as substrate incorporation, respiration or product formation. | State the assay, substrate and duration. Activity can occur without division and may be restricted to a small fraction of cells. |
| **survival** | Persistence through an exposure followed by recovery or retained viability. | Survival at a temperature does not show activity at that temperature; recovery during warming must be distinguished from in situ function. |
| **detection** | Observation of cells, DNA, RNA, proteins, lipids or another biomarker. | Detection alone establishes neither viability nor activity. Ancient or extracellular material and contamination are particular concerns. |

Use **viability** only for evidence that cells remain capable of biological function or recovery under a defined assay. Culturability is one operational measure of viability, not its synonym. Avoid **dormant** unless a reversible low-activity state is supported rather than assumed.

## 4. Evidence organised by chapter section

## 1. Life at low temperature: definitions and limits

### Verified scientific claims

- Morita's cardinal-temperature definition remains the clearest historical convention, but psychrophily is better understood as ecological and physiological adaptation along a continuum [@morita1975; @cavicchioli2016].
- Growth at subzero temperature requires a liquid phase. In laboratory media this can be maintained by salts or other solutes; in ice and frozen soils, organisms may occupy brines, pores or interfacial liquid films [@deming2002].
- *P. halocryophilus* Or1 showed growth at −15 °C in Trypticase soy broth supplemented with 18% NaCl and 7% glycerol. Growth was slow, with an estimated generation time near 50 days, and cultures reached much lower cell densities than at the optimum [@mykytczuk2013].
- In a separate assay, inoculated sterile-permafrost microcosms mineralised radiolabelled acetate at temperatures down to −25 °C over 210 days. This is evidence of respiration or metabolic activity, not growth at −25 °C [@mykytczuk2013].
- Natural permafrost communities incorporated radiolabelled acetate into lipids at −20 °C, showing measurable activity under the assay conditions without identifying the active taxa or demonstrating reproduction [@rivkina2000].

### Qualifications

- “Lowest temperature” records are method- and medium-dependent. Solute composition, water activity, phase state, inoculum history, incubation time and detection limit must accompany the number.
- The *P. halocryophilus* medium combined cold stress with 18% NaCl and glycerol; its result is an example of growth under coupled cold–solute conditions, not a temperature-only limit.
- A 50-day generation estimate depends on the growth model and measurement quality. Use “about 50 days under the reported conditions”, not a universal organismal property.
- Current evidence does not justify a universal lower temperature for life, nor does it establish reproduction at −20 or −25 °C.

### Representative evidence

- Morita 1975 for operational terminology [@morita1975].
- Cavicchioli 2016 for ecological qualification [@cavicchioli2016].
- Mykytczuk et al. 2013 for the strongest controlled lower-growth case retained here [@mykytczuk2013].
- Rivkina et al. 2000 for environmental-community activity at −20 °C [@rivkina2000].

### Representative systems

- ***Planococcus halocryophilus* Or1:** use as an evidence case, explicitly separating liquid-culture growth at −15 °C from permafrost-microcosm respiration at −25 °C.

### Material to avoid

- “Life grows from −15 to 150 °C.”
- “*P. halocryophilus* grows at −25 °C.”
- “DNA or viable cells in ancient permafrost prove continuous metabolism.”
- “Psychrophiles are organisms whose optimum is always below 15 °C” without stating that this is an operational convention.
- Unqualified global percentages for the area or volume of the cold biosphere.

### Figure opportunities

- Evidence ladder: detection → survival/viability → maintenance/activity → net growth → observed division.
- Ice/brine cross-section showing that subzero cells occupy liquid microhabitats rather than homogeneous solid ice.

## 2. What low temperature does to microbial cells

### Verified scientific claims

- Lower temperature generally slows thermally activated reactions by reducing the fraction of molecular configurations that cross activation barriers. This is a kinetic effect and does not mean reactions become thermodynamically unfavourable [@damico2006].
- Cooling increases the viscosity of liquid water and slows diffusion and transport; the effect becomes spatially heterogeneous in porous frozen matrices and concentrated brines [@deming2002; @demaayer2014].
- Cooling increases lipid ordering and can move membranes towards gel-like states, compromising permeability control, transporter activity and membrane-associated energy conservation [@damico2006; @demaayer2014].
- Low temperature stabilises many nucleic-acid secondary structures and slows transcription, translation, folding and assembly processes [@damico2006; @lim2000].
- Freezing excludes most solutes from the ice lattice, concentrating ions and other solutes in the remaining liquid phase. Cells therefore encounter low temperature together with osmotic, ionic and water-availability constraints [@deming2002].
- Extracellular ice can dehydrate and mechanically constrain cells; intracellular ice is usually more damaging. Whether freezing occurs, and where, depends on nucleation and cooling history.

### Qualifications

- Arrhenius behaviour is a useful local model, not a complete description across phase transitions, acclimation or changing rate-limiting steps.
- Viscosity, diffusion and water activity depend on solution composition; do not attach a single multiplier to “cold water” without specified conditions.
- Greater oxygen solubility at low temperature does not by itself demonstrate greater cellular oxidative stress. Omit a general ROS claim unless supported by a specific system.
- “Cold denaturation” is a real protein phenomenon, but its biological importance varies; it should not replace the more general problem of slow catalysis and folding.

### Representative evidence

- D'Amico et al. 2006 and De Maayer et al. 2014 for integrated cellular consequences [@damico2006; @demaayer2014].
- Deming 2002 for phase state, brines and environmental coupling [@deming2002].

### Representative systems

No organism is needed in this section. The causal physical model should lead; systems appear with the adaptations they demonstrate.

### Material to avoid

- “Reaction rate is proportional to temperature.”
- “Cold changes thermodynamics by reducing available energy” without specifying the quantity or reaction.
- A full Arrhenius derivation.
- A flat list of damage mechanisms without causal links.

### Figure opportunities

- Core causal diagram: cooling → slower kinetics/increased viscosity/phase change/solute exclusion → transport, membrane, macromolecular and water-availability problems.

## 3. How cells adapt to cold

### Membranes and transport

#### Verified scientific claims

- Many cold-adapted bacteria alter lipid composition to keep membranes in a functional physical state. Documented responses include changes in unsaturation, chain length, branching and lipid class; the combination is organism- and condition-specific [@damico2006; @demaayer2014].
- Archaeal cold adaptation cannot be described with bacterial fatty-acid rules. In *Methanococcoides burtonii*, direct lipid analysis found greater unsaturation of ether-linked phospholipids at 4 °C than at 23 °C, demonstrating one archaeal route to temperature-dependent membrane adjustment [@nichols2004].
- Membrane adjustment supports permeability control and the operation of transport and energy-conserving complexes; it does not aim to maximise fluidity without limit.

#### Qualifications

- “More unsaturated fatty acids” is common but not universal, and it does not describe ether-linked archaeal lipids adequately.
- The direction of the branched-to-straight-chain ratio is not universal. In *P. halocryophilus*, the reported ratio fell at the lowest temperatures, warning against treating one index as a rule [@mykytczuk2013].
- Pigments and carotenoids may affect membrane properties or oxidative/photochemical stress in particular organisms. Do not present pigmentation as a universal cold adaptation.

#### Representative evidence

- Nichols et al. 2004 for direct archaeal lipid measurements [@nichols2004].
- Mykytczuk et al. 2013 as a counterexample to simplistic membrane recipes [@mykytczuk2013].

#### Representative systems

- ***Methanococcoides burtonii*:** a concise case preventing a bacteria-only treatment and showing temperature-dependent unsaturation in archaeal ether lipids.

#### Material to avoid

- Universal lipid formulas, especially “shorter, polyunsaturated fatty acids in all psychrophiles”.
- Treating archaeal membranes as bacterial membranes with different names.
- Unverified claims about trans-unsaturation or carotenoid abundance.

#### Figure opportunities

- Membrane-state diagram showing several alternative compositional adjustments, with bacterial and archaeal examples separated.

### Proteins, enzymes and metabolic flux

#### Verified scientific claims

- Cold-active enzyme homologues can have greater catalytic efficiency at low temperature than mesophilic homologues. Adaptation frequently reduces activation enthalpy and changes conformational dynamics around catalytic regions [@feller2003].
- In well-studied enzyme families, weak or fewer stabilising interactions can increase mobility required for catalysis, with trade-offs that may include lower thermal stability or weaker substrate affinity [@feller2003].
- The relevant change may be local rather than a uniformly more flexible protein. Cold-active proteins can retain high overall stability while having flexible active-site regions.
- Chaperones, folding catalysts and proteases can support folding and quality control at low temperature, but their expression patterns are not universal signatures of psychrophily [@damico2006; @demaayer2014].

#### Qualifications

- Activity, stability and flexibility are multi-dimensional measurements; “more flexible” is not a sufficient mechanistic explanation.
- A trade-off demonstrated by mutagenesis in one enzyme family is a strong example, not a universal law.
- Higher low-temperature activity need not imply a higher *k*cat under every assay condition; compare homologues at matched temperatures and state the kinetic quantity.
- Amino-acid composition trends have many exceptions and should not be taught as a predictive recipe.

#### Representative evidence

- Feller and Gerday 2003 for kinetic and structural synthesis, including mutational evidence [@feller2003].
- D'Amico et al. 2006 for the cell-level connection [@damico2006].

#### Representative systems

- Use one enzyme family from Feller and Gerday's synthesis, preferably chitobiase, only to demonstrate experimentally that stabilising mutations can reduce cold catalytic performance while improving stability or binding. Do not add a catalogue of enzyme structures.

#### Material to avoid

- “All cold-active enzymes are globally flexible and unstable.”
- “Cold enzymes are ten orders of magnitude more active.”
- Lists of proline, glycine, arginine or salt-bridge “rules”.
- Equating purified-enzyme activity with organismal growth rate.

#### Figure opportunities

- Energy-landscape diagram showing a lower activation enthalpy and the possible stability/affinity cost, labelled as a common model rather than a law.

### Nucleic acids and gene expression

#### Verified scientific claims

- Stable RNA secondary structures at low temperature can impede translation initiation, ribosome biogenesis and RNA turnover [@damico2006; @lim2000].
- DEAD-box proteins remodel RNA–RNA and RNA–protein complexes using ATP; low-temperature regulation of an archaeal DEAD-box helicase was demonstrated in *M. burtonii* [@lim2000].
- Cold-shock proteins can bind nucleic acids and assist gene expression during cooling. A transient cold-shock response in a mesophile, acclimation over days and constitutive adaptation in a psychrophile are distinct phenomena [@demaayer2014].
- Transcriptomics and comparative genomics can identify candidate responses. Functional claims require additional biochemical, genetic or physiological tests.

#### Qualifications

- Lim et al. established low-temperature regulation and biochemical properties, while the exact organism-level fitness contribution was not established by a knockout in that study [@lim2000].
- “RNA helicase” is useful shorthand, but DEAD-box proteins often remodel short duplexes or ribonucleoprotein complexes rather than processively unwinding long RNA.
- A gene induced by cold may mediate general stress, acclimation or growth-rate effects rather than evolutionary cold adaptation.

#### Representative evidence

- Lim et al. 2000 for a defined archaeal system [@lim2000].
- De Maayer et al. 2014 for the wider mechanistic context [@demaayer2014].

#### Representative systems

- Reuse ***M. burtonii*** briefly for its low-temperature-regulated DEAD-box helicase; this strengthens the archaeal thread without adding another organism.

#### Material to avoid

- “Psychrophiles linearise their mRNA.”
- Gene lists and claims that cold-enriched genes are diagnostic of cold adaptation.
- Horizontal gene transfer claims without phylogenetic and functional evidence.

#### Figure opportunities

- RNA hairpin/ribonucleoprotein schematic showing local remodelling, coupled to an evidence chain from expression to functional validation.

### Ice, compatible solutes and extracellular microenvironments

#### Verified scientific claims

- Ice-binding proteins adsorb to ice and can alter crystal growth, recrystallisation or ice adhesion. Ice-nucleating proteins instead promote nucleation at warmer subzero temperatures. These functions are not two subclasses of “antifreeze” [@dolev2016].
- In *M. primoryensis*, antibody-blocking and microfluidic experiments directly showed that a surface-associated protein mediates adhesion of motile cells to ice [@bardolev2016]. Structural work established that this activity is one region of a very large multidomain adhesin [@guo2017].
- Compatible solutes can protect macromolecules and balance osmotic stress without broadly disrupting cellular chemistry. Their importance in frozen habitats follows partly from solute concentration in the remaining liquid phase [@deming2002; @demaayer2014].
- EPS are chemically heterogeneous extracellular matrices. In *C. psychrerythraea* 34H, EPS production increased under some extreme-temperature and pressure conditions, and native EPS was preferentially retained relative to salts in laboratory-grown saline ice [@marx2009; @ewert2011].

#### Qualifications

- An ice-binding assay does not by itself show freezing avoidance, freeze tolerance or a fitness benefit in nature.
- The modern interpretation of the *M. primoryensis* protein is primarily an ice adhesin, not a soluble antifreeze that protects cells by strongly depressing the freezing point [@bardolev2016; @guo2017].
- In the *Colwellia* ice-retention experiment, the heat-labile active component was not identified; a protein contribution or conformational change remained possible [@ewert2011].
- EPS may retain water, bind solutes, protect extracellular enzymes and structure biofilms, but no single preparation demonstrates all functions.

#### Representative evidence

- Dolev et al. 2016 for ice-binding terminology and mechanism [@dolev2016].
- Bar-Dolev et al. 2016 and Guo et al. 2017 for direct adhesion evidence [@bardolev2016; @guo2017].
- Marx et al. 2009 and Ewert and Deming 2011 for EPS [@marx2009; @ewert2011].

#### Representative systems

- ***Marinomonas primoryensis*:** direct demonstration that an ice-binding domain can serve adhesion and positioning rather than simply act as antifreeze.
- ***Colwellia psychrerythraea* 34H:** extracellular-microenvironment case, used with explicit limits on what its EPS experiments prove.

#### Material to avoid

- “Antifreeze proteins consist of avoidance and tolerance classes.”
- “EPS contains glycerol, glucose and DMSO as a general rule.”
- “All psychrophiles make EPS to depress the freezing point.”
- Inferring a named protein from bulk protein concentration in an uncharacterised supernatant.

#### Figure opportunities

- Comparative mechanism panel: ice adhesion, ice-growth inhibition, ice nucleation, intracellular compatible solutes and EPS-mediated microhabitat effects.

## 4. Cold never acts alone

### Verified scientific claims

- Freezing couples temperature to salinity and water availability because ice formation concentrates solutes in the residual liquid [@deming2002]. Cold–salt interaction is therefore intrinsic to sea ice, many frozen soils and the *P. halocryophilus* growth experiment [@mykytczuk2013].
- Deep-ocean organisms experience low temperature and high hydrostatic pressure together. Both variables affect membranes, proteins and rates; their combined effects cannot be inferred by adding two independent stress lists [@deming2002].
- Compatible-solute transport, membrane adjustment and EPS may contribute to more than one stress response, complicating assignment of a trait as uniquely “cold adaptive” [@demaayer2014].

### Qualifications

- The chapter should explain the interaction, not detailed halophile or piezophile physiology.
- Laboratory “cold” treatments that also change salt, glycerol or nutrient state cannot attribute every response to temperature.
- Radiation, desiccation and oligotrophy are optional modifiers, not reasons to create named physiotype categories.

### Representative evidence

- Deming 2002 for cold–salt–pressure coupling [@deming2002].
- Mykytczuk et al. 2013 for a controlled but compound cold–solute system [@mykytczuk2013].

### Representative systems

- Reuse ***P. halocryophilus* Or1** to show that the strongest lower-growth case is also a polyextreme experiment.
- Use a generic deep-sea cell only in a conceptual comparison; no additional named psychropiezophile is necessary.

### Material to avoid

- Baro-, halo-, radio- and troglo-psychrophile catalogues.
- *Deinococcus radiodurans* as a generic psychrophile.
- Treating salinity, NaCl concentration, ionic strength and water activity as interchangeable.

### Figure opportunities

- Two-row interaction matrix: freezing → solute concentration/water-activity change; depth → cold plus hydrostatic pressure. Link each to membranes, proteins and transport.

## 5. Ecology and evolution

### Verified scientific claims

- Cold-adapted microorganisms occur across Bacteria, Archaea and microbial Eukarya; psychrophily is a physiological and ecological strategy, not a taxonomic group [@lauro2013].
- Permanently and seasonally cold habitats differ in liquid-water regime, resource supply, salinity, pressure and disturbance. These differences help explain why no universal psychrophile adaptation package exists [@deming2002; @lauro2013].
- Active cold-adapted microorganisms contribute to local carbon and nutrient transformations, but the responsible taxa, rates and ecosystem importance must be established for each environment.
- Similar functional outcomes—membrane adjustment, cold-active catalysis or RNA remodelling—can arise through different molecular routes. “Repeated evolutionary solutions” is defensible at this functional level; independent origins of a particular trait require phylogenetic evidence.

### Qualifications

- Do not infer ecological activity from sequence abundance alone.
- Avoid a claim that psychrophiles constitute a quantified fraction of global biomass unless the denominator and evidence are explicit.
- Environment-specific food webs, climate feedbacks and permafrost carbon dynamics belong in environment chapters.

### Representative evidence

- Lauro and Cavicchioli 2013 for broad ecology and distribution [@lauro2013].
- Deming 2002 for polar liquid microhabitats and coupled constraints [@deming2002].
- The four mechanistic systems retained elsewhere provide sufficient taxonomic breadth for this chapter.

### Representative systems

Do not add a separate organism set. Reconnect *P. halocryophilus*, *M. burtonii*, *M. primoryensis* and *C. psychrerythraea* to the habitats and selective problems already introduced.

### Material to avoid

- Genus catalogues or an unexplained 16S rRNA tree.
- Claims that every cold-adaptive trait evolved independently.
- Greenland bioalbedo, polar food webs and permafrost thaw as extended case studies.

### Figure opportunities

- No standalone habitat map. If a visual is needed, use a compact habitat-to-constraint table that cross-references environment chapters.

## 6. Research frontiers and broader significance

### Verified scientific claims

- The lower temperature for sustained reproduction remains contingent on liquid phase, chemical conditions, timescale and assay sensitivity; it is not a settled universal constant.
- Functional validation is the major gap between comparative omics and mechanistic understanding. Strong evidence progresses from association to perturbation, biochemical function and organismal phenotype.
- The generality and physical basis of the activity–stability–flexibility model remain active questions, especially the relative importance of local and global dynamics.
- Cold-active enzymes can provide useful activity at lower process temperatures, while heat lability can allow easy inactivation. These properties justify brief application examples, not assumptions about commercial deployment [@feller2003].
- Rahman et al. found that extracellular supernatants from three cold-grown bacterial cultures altered freezing/thawing measurements in a frost-susceptible soil system [@rahman2024]. The experiment supports a supernatant effect; it did not identify the active molecules or demonstrate field-scale frost control.

### Qualifications

- “Potential application” and “commercially used” are different claims.
- Energy savings require a process-level comparison, not merely an enzyme with activity at low temperature.
- The Rahman case should appear only if the chapter benefits from a cautionary frontier example; it is not needed to establish core cold adaptation.

### Representative evidence

- Mykytczuk et al. 2013 for limits and the ambiguity of compound stress [@mykytczuk2013].
- Feller and Gerday 2003 for enzyme questions [@feller2003].
- Rahman et al. 2024 for the optional frontier case [@rahman2024].

### Representative systems

- No new organism. Use one cold-active enzyme and, optionally, the Rahman soil-supernatant system.

### Material to avoid

- Lists of antimicrobials, nanoparticles, pharmaceuticals, detergents, foods, bioremediation processes and planetary uses.
- Unverified statements that a named psychrophile enzyme is commercially deployed.
- Mars or Europa metabolism scenarios.

### Figure opportunities

- Evidence-to-inference ladder: gene presence → expression → biochemical activity → perturbation phenotype → environmental function.
- Applications need at most a small property → process advantage → limitation table.

## 7. Key concepts

The evidence supports the five approved concepts without amendment:

1. Low temperature changes kinetics, viscosity, phase behaviour and macromolecular dynamics.
2. Adaptations solve specific physicochemical problems and impose trade-offs.
3. Psychrophile categories are operational; laboratory optima do not directly establish environmental fitness.
4. Detection, survival, activity and growth are distinct evidence levels.
5. Natural cold habitats are multi-stressor systems.

## 7. Figure evidence

### Figure 1 — What does a lower-temperature record demonstrate?

- **Question addressed:** Why are detection, survival, activity and reproduction not interchangeable?
- **Visual logic:** An ascending evidence ladder, with assay examples and a split *P. halocryophilus* panel: growth at −15 °C in liquid medium versus respiration at −25 °C in frozen microcosms.
- **Supporting evidence:** Mykytczuk et al. 2013 and Rivkina et al. 2000 [@mykytczuk2013; @rivkina2000].
- **Confidence:** High for the hierarchy; moderate–high for interpreting the −25 °C result as low activity.
- **Pedagogical purpose:** Prevent the most consequential error in the lecture material.

### Figure 2 — From cooling to cellular consequences

- **Question addressed:** How does low temperature generate several linked biological problems?
- **Visual logic:** Cooling branches to slower kinetics, higher viscosity, membrane ordering and freezing/solute exclusion; branches reconverge on transport, catalysis, gene expression and water availability.
- **Supporting evidence:** D'Amico et al. 2006, De Maayer et al. 2014 and Deming 2002 [@damico2006; @demaayer2014; @deming2002].
- **Confidence:** High qualitatively.
- **Pedagogical purpose:** Provide the causal mental model for the chapter without re-teaching physical chemistry.

### Figure 3 — Different solutions to different cellular problems

- **Question addressed:** Why is there no universal psychrophile adaptation package?
- **Visual logic:** Four matched rows: membrane ordering → lipid adjustment; slow catalysis → altered enzyme dynamics; stable RNA structures → RNA remodelling; ice/brine exposure → IBP, compatible-solute or EPS mechanisms. Each ends with a limitation or trade-off.
- **Supporting evidence:** Nichols et al. 2004, Feller and Gerday 2003, Lim et al. 2000, Bar-Dolev et al. 2016 and Ewert and Deming 2011 [@nichols2004; @feller2003; @lim2000; @bardolev2016; @ewert2011].
- **Confidence:** High for the general causal matches; moderate for generalised EPS benefit.
- **Pedagogical purpose:** Replace an adaptation catalogue with functional reasoning.

### Figure 4 — The liquid microenvironment in saline ice

- **Question addressed:** How does freezing make cold a polyextreme condition?
- **Visual logic:** Ice crystals exclude solutes into brine channels; a cell and EPS occupy the liquid phase; arrows show falling temperature, increasing concentration and decreasing liquid fraction. Avoid unsupported numerical scales.
- **Supporting evidence:** Deming 2002, Marx et al. 2009 and Ewert and Deming 2011 [@deming2002; @marx2009; @ewert2011].
- **Confidence:** High for solute exclusion and liquid channels; moderate for specific EPS effects.
- **Pedagogical purpose:** Make cold–salinity coupling visible and prevent the image of uniform life inside solid ice.

## 8. Representative case studies

### 1. *Planococcus halocryophilus* Or1 — what a lower-limit experiment proves

**Teaching purpose:** Separate growth from activity and expose the importance of medium, phase and timescale. It deserves inclusion because a single paper reports both a strong culture-growth result and a distinct lower-temperature respiration result, making the evidence hierarchy concrete [@mykytczuk2013]. Do not use its transcriptome as proof that every induced gene is adaptive.

### 2. *Methanococcoides burtonii* — an archaeal route to cold adaptation

**Teaching purpose:** Prevent a bacteria-only membrane account and link membrane and RNA modules. Direct lipid analysis supports temperature-dependent unsaturation of archaeal ether lipids [@nichols2004], while a low-temperature-regulated DEAD-box helicase provides a bounded gene-expression example [@lim2000]. Do not generalise either response to all Archaea.

### 3. *Marinomonas primoryensis* — ice binding as adhesion

**Teaching purpose:** Correct the simplistic “antifreeze protein” narrative. Direct blocking and microfluidic experiments show cell adhesion to ice through MpIBP, while structural work explains how an ice-binding domain sits within a giant multidomain adhesin [@bardolev2016; @guo2017]. This is a mechanistically complete example connecting molecule, cell behaviour and habitat positioning.

### 4. *Colwellia psychrerythraea* 34H — extracellular microhabitat modification

**Teaching purpose:** Show that adaptation can extend beyond the cell. EPS production and experimental ice retention provide plausible routes to structuring an icy microenvironment [@marx2009; @ewert2011]. The case deserves inclusion only with the caveat that EPS is heterogeneous and the active ice-interacting component was not identified.

## 9. Remaining uncertainties

### Scientific uncertainty

- Whether −15 °C remains the lowest robustly demonstrated temperature for net microbial growth under a definition acceptable to the editor; the targeted search found no stronger primary case, but this is a record claim and should remain dated and qualified.
- Whether a meaningful universal lower temperature for reproduction exists independently of phase state, solute chemistry and observation time.
- How general the activity–stability–flexibility model is across enzyme families, and when local dynamics rather than global flexibility explain cold activity.
- How multiple constraints interact non-additively in intact cells and natural communities.
- Which extracellular ice effects provide measurable fitness advantages under natural conditions.

### Insufficient evidence

- A general causal link between greater oxygen solubility and oxidative stress in psychrophiles.
- Universal costs of membrane, RNA, compatible-solute and EPS adaptations.
- Functional validation for many gene families labelled “cold-adaptive” by comparative genomics or differential expression.
- Identification of the heat-labile component responsible for *Colwellia* EPS retention in saline ice.
- Identification of the active compounds and mechanism in the Rahman et al. soil-supernatant system.
- Verified commercial deployment and whole-process energy savings for a chapter-level application example.

### Editorial decisions

- Decide whether to call Morita-compliant organisms “psychrophiles” while using “cold-adapted microorganisms” more broadly; this package recommends that approach.
- Decide whether the approximate 50-day generation time at −15 °C is pedagogically useful or too method-dependent for the main text.
- Choose between the *Marinomonas* ice-adhesion case and the *Colwellia* EPS case if chapter length permits only one extracellular example.
- Decide whether Rahman et al. 2024 adds enough value as a frontier box. It should be omitted unless its evidential ambiguity is itself the teaching point.
- Confirm the final title and location of `Extremophiles' contributions to society` for cross-referencing applications.

## 10. Writing instructions

### Do

- Follow the approved architecture exactly.
- Begin with operational definitions and the evidence hierarchy, then reason from physical consequences to cellular problems and matched adaptations.
- State experimental conditions whenever a lower-limit claim appears.
- Use the four representative systems only where they teach a mechanism; reuse systems across subsections when that improves continuity.
- Present membrane and enzyme adaptations as alternative, lineage-specific solutions with costs or limitations.
- Distinguish ice adhesion, ice-growth inhibition, nucleation, compatible solutes and EPS.
- Integrate methods at the point where they determine what can be inferred.
- Keep applications to one short, mechanism-led subsection.

### Avoid

- Writing a literature review, taxonomic inventory, habitat catalogue or gene list.
- Treating Morita's thresholds as an immutable definition.
- Reporting −25 °C as a growth temperature for *P. halocryophilus*.
- Treating transcriptomic or comparative-genomic association as functional proof.
- Universal lipid recipes, amino-acid recipes or claims that all cold-active enzymes are globally flexible.
- Treating all ice-binding proteins as antifreezes or all EPS as cryoprotectants.
- Unverified global cold-area percentages, life-limit graphics, climate-feedback narratives, astrobiology scenarios or commercial claims.

### Remember

- The chapter's job is to teach a causal mental model, not maximise coverage.
- Detection, survival, maintenance, metabolic activity, growth and reproduction are never interchangeable.
- Cold is a condition imposed through a particular liquid phase and chemical environment, not an isolated scalar.
- Adaptations are hypotheses until the relevant function and organismal consequence are demonstrated.
- Scientific uncertainty should remain visible wherever the evidence does not distinguish competing interpretations.

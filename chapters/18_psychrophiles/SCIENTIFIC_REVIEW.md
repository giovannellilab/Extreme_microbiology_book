# Executive summary

The chapter is scientifically strong overall. It reconstructs the approved scope faithfully, maintains the essential distinctions among detection, survival, maintenance, activity, growth and reproduction, and avoids most common overgeneralisations about membranes, enzymes, omics and extracellular protection. Confidence in this assessment is high because the chapter's principal claims can be traced directly to the curated Evidence Package and its primary or authoritative sources.

One local factual error must be corrected: the membrane comparison reported for *Planococcus halocryophilus* is described as a ratio of branched to saturated fatty acids, whereas the Evidence Package identifies a branched-to-straight-chain ratio. Branched and saturated are not mutually exclusive categories. Four issues require important qualification: the evidential endpoints are presented too much like a strictly nested hierarchy; ice nucleation is assigned a possible adaptive benefit without sufficient system-specific qualification; all natural cold habitats are characterised as polyextreme; and occurrence in refrigerated systems is attributed specifically to psychrophiles despite the importance of psychrotolerant organisms and psychrotrophs in that setting. Two additional citation or reporting issues are minor.

The central scientific conclusions and chapter architecture remain sound. The necessary changes are local and should not require structural rewriting, but the Critical and Major findings should be resolved before publication. The recommendation is therefore **Major revision**, followed by targeted scientific re-review of the corrected statements.

# Positive observations

1. The −15 °C growth and −25 °C activity results for *P. halocryophilus* are separated accurately, with medium, duration and endpoint qualifications.
2. Membrane adaptation is correctly presented as lineage- and condition-specific, including an archaeal ether-lipid example.
3. The activity–stability–flexibility model is treated as a common, non-universal model involving local as well as global dynamics.
4. Omics associations are consistently distinguished from biochemical function, perturbation evidence and organismal phenotype.
5. Ice binding, ice nucleation, compatible solutes and EPS are separated mechanistically, and the *Marinomonas* and *Colwellia* cases retain appropriate evidential limits.

# Priority corrections

1. Correct the *P. halocryophilus* membrane-composition ratio after checking the primary study.
2. Remove the implication that all evidence endpoints form a strict linear hierarchy while preserving their non-equivalence.
3. Qualify the proposed organismal benefit of ice nucleation and support it with mechanism-appropriate evidence.
4. Replace the categorical claim that natural cold habitats are polyextreme with a qualified statement about commonly interacting constraints.
5. Resolve the conflation of psychrophiles with psychrotolerant or psychrotrophic organisms in engineered refrigeration.

# Publication recommendation

**Major revision.** The chapter's central argument is scientifically sound, and no architectural change is required. One factual error and four interpretation or terminology issues nevertheless require correction before publication because they concern core topics: membrane adaptation, evidential inference, ice interactions, polyextremophily and psychrophile classification.
